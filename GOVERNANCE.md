# Governance

YAWN is currently a maintainer-led open-source project. David Forman
([`@NestheadsDave`](https://github.com/NestheadsDave)) is the founding
maintainer and final steward of releases, repository access, and the project's
constitutional boundaries.

This is an honest description of present authority, not a claim of broad
community consensus. Governance should evolve when sustained contributors and
real interoperability create a reason to distribute it.

## How decisions are made

- Documentation, fixtures, and compatible implementation improvements may be
  accepted through ordinary pull-request review.
- Normative protocol changes use the [RFC process](rfcs/README.md).
- Durable architectural choices receive an [ADR](adr/README.md).
- Breaking changes require compatibility analysis, migration guidance, tests,
  and an explicit version decision.
- Security fixes may be prepared privately and merged before public rationale.
- The maintainer may reject a change that violates privacy, attribution,
  authority, or the human-agency boundary even when it is technically elegant.

## Roles

- **User**: tries the protocol and contributes lived evidence or feedback.
- **Contributor**: authors issues, docs, examples, code, schemas, or research.
- **Reviewer**: evaluates a specific change for a declared domain.
- **Maintainer**: can accept changes and is accountable for repository health.
- **Steward**: holds final authority for constitutional and release decisions.

Roles are granted explicitly and can be revoked. AI systems may assist any role
operationally but do not hold independent merge or governance authority unless
a future policy explicitly grants and bounds it.

## Conflicts and appeals

Reviewers disclose material conflicts. A contributor may ask for a rationale or
reconsideration with new evidence. The project records important disagreements
rather than manufacturing consensus. Conduct and security reports use their
private processes.

## Changing governance

A governance change needs an RFC describing the proposed authority, selection
and removal process, affected assets, transition receipts, and safeguards
against capture. The current steward approves the transition until a later
adopted governance document says otherwise.
