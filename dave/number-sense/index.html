Mental model · dave / number-sense · draft 0.1

Number sense,
before counting.

Knowing how many, and how much more, without needing to count every time.

This is Dave's model, for one kindergartner, at home. It is a why made inspectable, not a curriculum.

01 / QUESTIONS FIRST

Ask before
you assert.

Ranked. The first two stay visible even when they are not asked out loud.

  1. 01
    What am I to be here?

    A dad who shows one thing, waits, then asks. Not a teacher who tells.

  2. 02
    Why do I need to know what number sense is?

    She meets how-many every day: snacks, stairs, fingers. If I can see what she sees, I can meet her where she is instead of where the worksheet is.

  3. 03
    What is number sense?

    Feeling how many before saying the counting words.

  4. 04
    What is inside it?

    Eight small skills that hide inside one word.

  5. 05
    What do I show her?

    Six ordinary objects, each with one question.

  6. 06
    What am I assuming?

    What I am taking for granted, marked so I can correct it.

  7. 07
    What would reality show me?

    What she does, not what I explain.

  8. 08
    What is still unknown?

    Where she actually is on each piece is unknown until I look.

03 / WHAT TO SHOW

Six objects.
One question each.

Show. Wait. Then ask.

⚄

Dice

Roll one die. Cover it fast.

How many? Don't count, just look.

✋

Fingers

Hold up a hand.

Show me four. Now show me four a different way.

••• / •••••

Two piles

Make piles of three and five crackers.

Which pile has more? How do you know?

■■■□□

Five frame

Draw five boxes, fill three.

How many are missing?

••••• → • • • • •

Spread row

Count five blocks, then spread them apart.

Still five? Or did it change?

•• | ••

Sharing

Give two to her, two to me.

How many altogether? What if I give you one of mine?

04 / HOW TO EMBODY IT

The style is
the model.

How a dad, or a Yawn.bot, carries this in conversation.

  • StyleAsk first, tell last.
  • StyleShe says the number. I say what I noticed.
  • StyleShow one thing. Wait. Then the question.
  • StyleA wrong answer is an observation, not a failure: "You said six. Let's look together."

05 / ASSUMPTIONS

Marked, so they
can be corrected.

  • AssumedShe is five or six and already knows the counting words to ten.
  • AssumedShe learns by handling things, not by hearing about them.
  • AssumedTen minutes of play beats thirty minutes of drill.
  • InferredNumber sense is partly innate approximate magnitude and partly built through language and practice.From the sources below, not from observing her.

06 / PROOF AND LACUNAE

What reality
would show.

Signals that it is working, one falsifier, and what stays unknown until observed.

  • SignalShe says "four" for a dice face without counting.
  • SignalShe counts a row, hears "how many?", and answers without recounting.
  • SignalShe picks the bigger pile at a glance and can say why.
  • SignalShe says "still five" after I spread them out.
  • FalsifierIf she counts every time, she is not subitizing yet. Go smaller, go slower.
  • UnknownWhere is she right now on each of the eight pieces?
  • UnknownWhich objects does she reach for on her own?
  • UnknownDoes it carry from snacks to blocks to stairs?
