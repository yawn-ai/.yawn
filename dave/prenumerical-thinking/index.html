Mental model · dave / prenumerical-thinking · draft 0.1

How much,
before numbers.

Reasoning about quantity, order, and pattern before the counting words carry meaning.

This is Dave's model, for one kindergartner, at home. It sits underneath number sense.

01 / QUESTIONS FIRST

Ask before
you assert.

Ranked. The first two stay visible even when they are not asked out loud.

  1. 01
    What am I to be here?

    The person who notices what she already does with how-much before any number word.

  2. 02
    Why do I need to know what prenumerical thinking is?

    Numbers land on top of this. If it is thin, counting to ten is a song without meaning. If it is solid, the numbers have somewhere to sit.

  3. 03
    What is prenumerical thinking?

    Handling how much with no number words: same, different, match, more, less, order, pattern.

  4. 04
    What is inside it?

    Seven moves she can make with her hands.

  5. 05
    What do I show her?

    Six household moments, each with one question.

  6. 06
    What am I assuming?

    What I am taking for granted, marked so I can correct it.

  7. 07
    What would reality show me?

    What she does with objects, not what she says about them.

  8. 08
    What is still unknown?

    Which of these she already has is unknown until I watch.

03 / WHAT TO SHOW

Six moments.
One question each.

Hand her the objects. She does; I narrate.

◐ ◑ ◐

Sock pile

Dump the clean socks out.

Find its match. How did you know?

○ ○ ○ / | | |

Table

Hand her the spoons at dinner.

One spoon for every bowl. Any left over?

▪ ■ ◼

Cups

Give her three nested cups, then five.

Line them up, smallest to biggest.

• • ▲

Clap

Clap, clap, stomp. Repeat it twice.

What comes next?

▂ ▅

Two juices

Pour two different amounts.

Which has more? Which one do you want?

[ ●●●● ]

Blocks in a bag

Let her feel a bag without looking.

A lot, or a little?

04 / HOW TO EMBODY IT

The style is
the model.

How a dad, or a Yawn.bot, carries this in conversation.

  • StyleUse the quantity words in ordinary moments. Do not call it a lesson.
  • StyleHand her the objects. She does; I narrate what she did.
  • StyleWhen she sorts by a rule I did not expect, ask for her rule before offering mine.
  • StyleNotice what is already there before trying to install anything.

05 / ASSUMPTIONS

Marked, so they
can be corrected.

  • AssumedThese moves come before and underneath counting.Piaget's ordering. Later work disputes how strict the prerequisite is.
  • AssumedShe already does most of this in play; my job is to notice, not to teach.
  • AssumedHousehold objects beat printed worksheets at this age.

06 / PROOF AND LACUNAE

What reality
would show.

Signals that it is working, one falsifier, and what stays unknown until observed.

  • SignalShe matches one-to-one without being told to.
  • SignalShe orders three cups by size, then five.
  • SignalShe continues an AB pattern, then an ABB pattern.
  • SignalShe says "more" and "all gone" about the right piles.
  • FalsifierIf she can chant to ten but cannot hand one cracker to each person, the words are ahead of the sense.
  • UnknownWhich of the seven moves does she already have?
  • DisputedMust conservation come before counting, or does it grow alongside it?
  • UnknownWhich moments does she start on her own?

07 / SOURCES

Reported,
and one dispute.

Piaget, The Child's Conception of Number (1952): classification, seriation, conservation. Gelman and Gallistel (1978): counting competence earlier than Piaget's prerequisites. Clements and Sarama, learning trajectories for early math.

The first two disagree on ordering. This model keeps both visible.
